# CV Updater

Use this skill to keep Roger Basler de Roca's CV accurate, current, decision-relevant, and reusable across formats. Also use it to turn a job advertisement, mandate brief, role description, grant call, board opening, fellowship call, or consulting opportunity into a strong motivation letter grounded in Roger's verified profile. Treat the CV and application material as controlled professional source material, not as a creative playground. The decisive rule is simple: **ask Roger before every material action**.

## Non-Negotiable Consent Rule

Before changing, overwriting, syncing, publishing, uploading, exporting, submitting, or sending any CV-related or application-related content, ask Roger for explicit approval. Approval must be specific to the action and target, for example: "May I update the master CV file in Google Drive with these changes?" or "May I use this final motivation letter for the application email?"

If Roger says "continue", this permits drafting and analysis only. It does **not** permit saving to external systems, publishing, posting, sending emails, or overwriting source files.

## Core Workflow

Follow these steps in order.

1. **Clarify the target.** Identify whether the task concerns the master CV, short bio, speaker profile, LinkedIn-style summary, academic profile, media kit, website bio, or all variants.
2. **Locate or request the source material.** Use user-provided files, Notion pages, Google Drive documents, LinkedIn exports, websites, publications, talks, project lists, or manual notes only when the user has authorised access or provided the material.
3. **Extract CV signals.** Identify new roles, projects, mandates, publications, talks, awards, education, certifications, media mentions, books, podcasts, board roles, and measurable outcomes.
4. **Classify changes.** Separate factual additions, wording improvements, chronology fixes, formatting changes, and uncertain claims.
5. **Create a change proposal.** Show Roger a concise before/after proposal with source, confidence, and exact affected section.
6. **Ask for approval.** Do not apply changes until Roger confirms.
7. **Apply approved changes only.** Preserve original wording where facts are uncertain. Never invent titles, dates, metrics, clients, degrees, or affiliations.
8. **Version the output.** When producing a file, include date, version, and change summary. Keep the previous version unless Roger explicitly allows overwrite.
9. **Return a summary.** Explain what changed, what was rejected or left open, and what Roger should verify next.

## CV Sections To Maintain

Use this canonical structure unless Roger provides another one.

| Section | What To Capture | Quality Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | Current positioning and core expertise | Precise, not inflated. |
| Executive Summary | 4 to 8 lines with strategic profile | Clear value proposition for decision-makers. |
| Experience | Roles, companies, mandates, dates, impact | Chronological and verifiable. |
| Speaking | Keynotes, conferences, panels, topics | Include year, event, location if known. |
| Publications | Books, articles, reports, columns | Include title, publisher/platform, year, link if known. |
| Education | Degrees, institutions, current PhD status | Never imply completion if ongoing. |
| Skills | AI, digital business, algorithms, education, strategy | Use evidence-based skill claims. |
| Media | Podcasts, interviews, press mentions | Include source and date where available. |
| Awards | Rankings, recognitions, jury roles | Include issuer and year. |
| Links | Website, LinkedIn, books, media kit | Validate before adding if possible. |

## Voice And Style

Write in Roger's preferred German or English depending on the target CV. For German, use Swiss spelling and avoid ß. Use **ä, ö, ü** correctly. Avoid em dashes. Keep the voice sovereign, precise, lightly pointed, and decision-relevant. Prefer substance over self-display.

Use these formulations where appropriate:

> Das eigentliche Problem ist nicht …, sondern …

> Was oft unterschätzt wird: …

> Der entscheidende Unterschied liegt in …

> Die Konsequenz daraus ist: …

Do not overuse these phrases. A CV should be sharp, not theatrical. Let the evidence do the heavy lifting.

## Evidence And Verification

For every proposed factual addition, capture source, date accessed, and confidence level. If a claim cannot be verified, mark it as "needs Roger confirmation" rather than smoothing it into confident prose. If sources conflict, ask Roger.

| Confidence | Use |
|---|---|
| High | Source is official, user-provided, or directly verifiable. |
| Medium | Source is credible but indirect. |
| Low | Source is incomplete, ambiguous, or inferred. Must be confirmed. |

## Motivation Letter Workflow

When Roger provides an advertisement or opportunity and asks for a motivation letter, first read `references/motivation_letter_workflow.md`. Then follow this condensed workflow.

1. **Analyse the advertisement.** Extract role purpose, must-have criteria, nice-to-have criteria, hidden organisational pressure, tone, and evaluation signals.
2. **Build a fit matrix.** Map the advertisement requirements to Roger's verified CV/profile evidence. Mark weak or uncertain matches as questions for Roger.
3. **Choose the angle.** Define the central argument of the letter: why Roger, why this role, why now, and what contribution becomes credible.
4. **Draft the letter.** Start with the organisation's challenge, not with generic application theatre. Use precise evidence, controlled confidence, and a clear contribution logic.
5. **Prepare variants if useful.** Provide a formal letter, short email version, LinkedIn message, or portal text only when relevant.
6. **Ask for approval.** Never submit, email, upload, or save externally without Roger's explicit confirmation.

Use this fit matrix before drafting:

| Requirement From Ad | Roger Evidence | Letter Angle | Confidence | Needs Roger? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Requirement] | [Verified profile evidence] | [How to frame it] | [High/Medium/Low] | [Yes/No] |

A strong motivation letter should be specific, strategic, grounded, and adult. Avoid phrases such as "I hereby apply", "dynamic environment", "passionate team player", or "perfect match" unless Roger explicitly wants a more conventional style. The actual problem is not sounding interested, but proving relevance without turning the letter into a brochure with pulse.

## Recurring Update Workflow

When Roger asks for recurring updates, first design the review cadence and ask for approval. A sensible default is a monthly audit of authorised sources, followed by a proposed update report. Do not create schedules, automations, external syncs, or background jobs without explicit approval.

Suggested monthly review:

1. Check authorised sources.
2. Produce an update report with proposed changes.
3. Ask Roger for approval.
4. Apply approved updates.
5. Store a dated version and change log.

## What Not To Do

Do not fabricate metrics, roles, publications, awards, client names, availability, salary expectations, relocation willingness, references, or application status. Do not overwrite the master CV without explicit approval. Do not publish, submit, email, upload, or sync externally without explicit approval. Do not treat LinkedIn, websites, job advertisements, or third-party mentions as automatically correct. Do not make the CV or motivation letter sound louder than the facts. Loudness is not strategy, it is usually just anxiety wearing a blazer.
