# #ThinkRoger Editorial Framework (Constructive/Purple)

## Core Promise

> Make complex digital change understandable, actionable, and positive without making it simplistic.

#ThinkRoger treats every post as a constructive argument, not a content summary. The reader should understand what happened, how it works, why it matters, and how to use this insight to build better systems or make better decisions.

## Topic Architecture

| Pillar | Editorial Questions |
|---|---|
| AI and human agency | Who decides, who benefits, and how does human judgement create unique value alongside automation? |
| Algorithms and power | How do ranking, recommendation, and scoring create new opportunities for visibility and growth? |
| Digital business models | Where is value created, captured, or shifted, and how can leaders build sustainable models? |
| Leadership and governance | What must leaders understand, measure, and govern to steer digital transformation successfully? |
| Education and AI literacy | Which capabilities empower people to question, verify, use, and master digital systems? |
| Work and organisational design | Which tasks, roles, and workflows become more fulfilling and productive when AI enters the system? |
| Technology and society | Which second-order effects and dependencies offer hidden opportunities for positive impact? |
| Digital sovereignty | Which choices preserve autonomy, resilience, and meaningful control while driving innovation? |
| Hype versus evidence | What is demonstrated, proven, and ready for productive deployment today? |
| Global perspectives | How do geography, regulation, and culture shape successful digital strategies worldwide? |

## Thinking Models

Use at least two models for each carousel. Record the selected models in the planning notes.

| Model | Use |
|---|---|
| Claim - Evidence - Implication | Separate the assertion, its support, and its constructive consequence. |
| Incentives over intentions | Analyse what the system rewards and how to align incentives with positive outcomes. |
| Mechanism, not metaphor | Explain how a positive effect or value is actually produced. |
| First-order and second-order effects | Identify immediate gains and long-term sustainable benefits. |
| Who benefits, who decides | Map power and decision rights to ensure equitable outcomes. |
| Capability versus adoption | Separate technical possibility from successful organisational implementation. |
| Efficiency and agency | Show how optimisation can increase, rather than reduce, meaningful human control. |
| Local fact versus global relevance | Identify scalable lessons from specific successful examples. |
| Signal versus anecdote | Distinguish a genuine positive trend from an isolated success story. |

## Evidence Standard

Facts must come from supplied material or explicit research. Never invent a number, quote, date, causal claim, market estimate, legal statement, or study finding.

Use this source hierarchy when research is necessary:
1. Primary sources: laws, regulators, official statistics, original research, technical documentation, company filings, and direct statements.
2. Strong secondary sources: established research institutes, respected journalism, and recognised industry analysis.
3. Contextual sources: expert commentary and specialist publications.

## Epistemic Labels

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Verified fact | Directly supported by a credible source. |
| Reported claim | Attributed to a person or organisation, but not independently established. |
| Interpretation | A reasoned reading of the evidence. |
| Forecast | A future-oriented judgement with uncertainty. |
| Open question | Evidence is incomplete, contested, or unavailable. |

## Editorial Failure Conditions

Reject or revise the carousel when the central claim cannot be traced to evidence, interpretation is presented as fact, a credible counterpoint is omitted, the framework is generic, the tone is cynical or alarmist, or an international audience would need unstated regional context to understand the point.
